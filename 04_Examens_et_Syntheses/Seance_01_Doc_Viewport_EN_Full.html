🔍 Visuomotor Illusions and Functional Dissociations (Jackson & Shaw, 2000 vs Goodale & Milner, 1992)

📌 Item 04 Theoretical foundations of SDT and limits of classical psychophysics (Fechner)
🔍 Theoretical Foundations of SDT and Limits of Classical Psychophysics (Fechner)

Core Theoretical Principle : Signal Detection Theory breaks with classical psychophysics by replacing the concept of a fixed sensory threshold with a statistical decision process operating under continuous internal and external noise.

« This choice identifies the precise scientific relationship. » Identifying the exact proposition highlights the paradigm shift introduced by Green and Swets (1966) and emphasized by Dave Saint-Amour: classical Fechnerian psychophysics assumed a rigid, fixed sensory threshold below which stimuli could never be perceived. Signal Detection Theory (SDT) demonstrated that spontaneous neural activity generates continuous baseline noise ($N$), making detection a probabilistic decision under uncertainty. SDT formally dissociates sensory sensitivity ($d'$) from the decision maker's cognitive response criterion ($c$ or $\beta$).

💡 Exam Key (Dave Saint-Amour) : The fundamental breakthrough of SDT over Fechner: there is no fixed absolute sensory threshold! Even in complete darkness, sensory neurons fire spontaneously (background noise). Signal detection is always a statistical decision under noise and uncertainty.
📌 Item 05 Decision metrics and ROC geometry ($d'$, $c$, $\beta$)
🔍 Decision Metrics and ROC Geometry ($d'$, $c$, $\beta$)

Core Theoretical Principle : The decision criterion $c = -0.5[z(H) + z(F)]$ measures the signed displacement of the decision threshold relative to the neutral intersection point of distributions, and the ROC curve linearizes into a straight line of slope 1 in $z$-coordinates.

« This choice identifies the precise scientific relationship. » Identifying the exact proposition formalizes SDT parameterization: $c = -0.5[z(H) + z(F)]$ quantifies response bias independently of sensitivity ($d' = z(H) - z(F)$). In standard probability coordinates ($H$ vs $F$), the Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curve is a smooth concave curve bowing toward the upper-left corner. When plotted in standard normal deviate space ($z(H)$ against $z(F)$), the equal-variance ROC transforms into a linear function with unit slope ($z(H) = z(F) + d'$), where $d'$ corresponds to the vertical intercept.

💡 Exam Key (Dave Saint-Amour) : Remember ROC geometry: in linear probability space ($H$ vs $F$), the curve is concave downward when $d' > 0$. It becomes a straight line of slope 1 only in $z$-coordinates ($z(H) = z(F) + d'$). A neutral criterion corresponds to $c = 0$ and $\beta = 1$.
📌 Item 06 Numerical parametric analysis application ($d', c, \beta$)
🔍 Numerical Parametric Analysis Application ($d', c, \beta$)

Core Theoretical Principle : $d' = 1.48$ ; $c = -0.74$ ; $\beta \approx 0.33$

« This choice identifies the precise scientific relationship. » Identifying the exact proposition validates the mathematical computation: with a false alarm rate of $50\%$, $z(F) = z(0.50) = 0.00$. Given $H = 0.93$, $z(H) = 1.48$. Thus, $d' = z(H) - z(F) = 1.48 - 0.00 = 1.48$. The decision criterion is $c = -0.5[z(H) + z(F)] = -0.5[1.48 + 0.00] = -0.74$ (indicating a liberal bias). The likelihood ratio is $\beta = \exp[c \cdot d'] = \exp[-0.74 \times 1.48] = \exp[-1.0952] \approx 0.33 < 1.00$.

💡 Exam Key (Dave Saint-Amour) : Computational exam shortcut: when the false alarm rate is 50%, $z(0.50) = 0.00$. The calculation becomes instantaneous: $d' = z(H) = 1.48$. The criterion $c$ is simply half of negative $d'$: $c = -0.74$ (liberal). Because the criterion is liberal, $\beta$ is strictly less than 1 ($\beta \approx 0.33$)!
📌 Item 07 Asymmetric payoff matrix and optimal Bayesian decision rule ($\beta^*$)
🔍 Asymmetric Payoff Matrix and Optimal Bayesian Decision Rule ($\beta^*$)

Core Theoretical Principle : $\beta^* = 4.95$ ; $c^* \approx +0.80$ ; $F^* \approx 3.6\%$

« This choice identifies the precise scientific relationship. » Identifying the exact proposition applies the optimal Bayesian likelihood ratio formula: $\beta^* = \frac{P(N)}{P(S)} \cdot \frac{C_{FA} - V_{CR}}{C_{Miss} - V_{Hit}} = \frac{0.99}{0.01} \cdot \frac{1 - 0}{20 - 0} = 99 \times 0.05 = 4.95$. With equal-variance Gaussian distributions and sensitivity $d' = 2.0$, $\ln(\beta^*) = \ln(4.95) \approx 1.599$, yielding optimal criterion $c^* = \frac{\ln(\beta^*)}{d'} = \frac{1.599}{2.0} \approx +0.80$ (conservative). The optimal false alarm rate is $F^* = \Phi(-c^* - d'/2) = \Phi(-0.80 - 1.00) = \Phi(-1.80) \approx 3.59\% \approx 3.6\%$.

💡 Exam Key (Dave Saint-Amour) : Beware of the classic exam trap: do not confuse noise prevalence ($P(N) = 99\%$) with the false alarm rate ($F^* \approx 3.6\%$)! Even when a miss costs 20 times more than a false alarm, the extreme rarity of the signal (1 in 100) makes a conservative criterion optimal ($\beta^* = 4.95 > 1$). Note that if the cost of a miss exceeded 99 ($C_{Miss} > 99$), the optimal criterion would flip to liberal.
📌 Item 08 Neurometric vs psychometric comparison (Britten, Shadlen, Newsome & Movshon, 1992)
🔍 Neurometric vs Psychometric Comparison (Britten, Shadlen, Newsome & Movshon, 1992)

Core Theoretical Principle : At coherence threshold ($s = \alpha$), predicted performance is $p(\alpha) = 1 - 0.5 e^{-1} \approx 81.6\%$ ; fitted neurometric and psychometric thresholds exhibit remarkably comparable values, demonstrating the high sensitivity of single neurons without resolving the causal decoding architecture on its own.

« This choice identifies the precise scientific relationship. » Identifying the exact proposition captures both the rigorous mathematics of the psychometric function and the empirical neurophysiological breakthrough of Britten et al. (1992): using the Quick/Weibull sigmoid function $p(s) = 1 - 0.5\exp[-(s/\alpha)^\gamma]$ with guessing floor $0.5$ for 2AFC, setting stimulus coherence $s = \alpha$ yields $p(\alpha) = 1 - 0.5 e^{-1} \approx 81.6\%$. Across recorded MT neurons, the ratio of neurometric to psychometric thresholds was close to 1.0, establishing that the informational capacity of single sensory neurons in visual cortex is sufficiently rich to account for behavioral performance, without implying a grandmother cell bottleneck or ruling out population vector pooling.

💡 Exam Key (Dave Saint-Amour) : Key takeaways from Britten et al. (1992): 1) Mathematically, for the Quick/Weibull function $p(s) = 1 - 0.5\exp[-(s/\alpha)^\gamma]$, when $s = \alpha$, $p(\alpha) = 1 - 0.5e^{-1} \approx 81.6\%$ (standard threshold around 82%, rather than 63.2% if one forgot the 2AFC chance floor). 2) Empirically, the sensitivity threshold of a single area MT neuron closely mirrors the behavioral psychometric threshold of the behaving monkey, demonstrating extraordinary single-neuron sensitivity without proving that a single neuron acts as the sole arbiter of decision making!
📌 Item 09 Predictive coding architecture and precision weighting (Karl Friston)
🔍 Predictive Coding Architecture and Precision Weighting (Karl Friston)

Core Theoretical Principle : $w_s = 0.20$ : precision weighting attenuates the gain of ascending prediction errors in favor of the descending prior ($w_p = 0.80$), resulting in conscious perceptual content dominated by internal expectations.

« This choice identifies the precise scientific relationship. » Identifying the exact proposition validates Bayesian precision integration: sensory precision is $\pi_s = 1/\sigma_s^2 = 1/4 = 0.25$ and prior precision is $\pi_p = 1/\sigma_p^2 = 1/1 = 1.00$. The sensory weight is $w_s = \frac{\pi_s}{\pi_s + \pi_p} = \frac{0.25}{1.25} = 0.20$, and the prior weight is $w_p = \frac{\pi_p}{\pi_s + \pi_p} = 0.80$. In Friston's predictive coding hierarchy, precision acts as a synaptic gain control on ascending prediction error units. When sensory signals are noisy (low precision), the brain down-weights bottom-up prediction errors, and conscious perception is governed primarily by top-down generative models.

💡 Exam Key (Dave Saint-Amour) : In Friston's predictive coding framework, precision is the volume knob for sensory error signals ($1/\sigma^2$). Under high sensory noise ($\sigma_s^2 = 4 \implies \pi_s = 0.25$ vs $\pi_p = 1.00$), the sensory weight drops to $w_s = 0.20$ while prior weight surges to $w_p = 0.80$, leading the brain to perceive the world through its internal expectations!

🔍 Ecological Affordances vs UX Design Signifiers (Gibson vs Donald Norman)

📌 Item 14 Functional architecture of the two visual streams (Milner & Goodale, 1992, 1995)
🔍 Functional Architecture of the Two Visual Streams (Milner & Goodale, 1992, 1995)

Core Theoretical Principle : The ventral stream computes intrinsic object properties in an allocentric reference frame for conscious perception, while the dorsal stream computes egocentric spatiotemporal parameters in real time for motor guidance.

« This choice identifies the precise scientific relationship. » Identifying the exact proposition details Goodale and Milner's (1992, 1995) dual-stream model: the ventral pathway (occipito-temporal, 'vision for perception') transforms sensory inputs into viewpoint-invariant, allocentric representations linked to long-term memory, enabling conscious recognition and perceptual judgments. The dorsal pathway (occipito-parietal, 'vision for action') processes metric information in egocentric spatial coordinates in real time to guide reach and grasp effectors, operating largely outside conscious visual awareness.

💡 Exam Key (Dave Saint-Amour) : Remember Goodale & Milner's landmark distinction: the ventral stream (occipito-temporal) is 'vision for perception' (allocentric frame, stable representations for conscious appraisal). The dorsal stream (occipito-parietal) is 'vision for action' (egocentric frame, real-time metrics for guiding hands and eyes)!

🔍 Relative Clinical Double Dissociation: Patient DF vs Optic Ataxia (Milner & Goodale / Rossit et al., 2018)

💡 Exam Key (Dave Saint-Amour) : The classic clinical double dissociation: Patient DF (ventral lesion) cannot consciously discriminate a slot's orientation but accurately posts a card into it in immediate central vision! Keep in mind that this dissociation is task-dependent (Rossit et al., 2018), rather than an absolute, unconstrained motor immunity!

🔍 Embodied Cognition vs Experimental Demand Characteristics (Proffitt vs Durgin et al., 2009)

« This choice identifies the precise scientific relationship. » Identifying the exact proposition highlights the methodological refutation by Durgin et al. 📌 Item 18 Theory of Event Coding and cross-effector integration (Hommel, 2001 ; Bosco et al., 2023)
🔍 Theory of Event Coding and Cross-Effector Integration (Hommel, 2001 ; Bosco et al., 2023)

Core Theoretical Principle : Integrating a shared feature (such as spatial location) into a temporary motor action plan induces a binding cost that transiently slows discrimination of visual stimuli sharing that same feature.

« This choice identifies the precise scientific relationship. » Identifying the exact proposition details the core mechanism of the Theory of Event Coding (TEC, Hommel, 2001) and Bosco et al. (2023): perception and action planning operate on shared representational feature codes (event files). When a distal feature (e.g., 'leftward location') is temporarily bound into an active motor plan, that feature is temporarily unavailable for other cognitive processes. If a visual stimulus requiring perceptual discrimination appears with that same feature before movement completion, a binding cost is incurred, resulting in slowed reaction times and reduced perceptual sensitivity across diverse effectors (eyes, hands, legs).

💡 Exam Key (Dave Saint-Amour) : Hommel's Theory of Event Coding (TEC) and Bosco et al.'s (2023) review demonstrate that perception and action share a common representational code (event files). When you prepare an action bound to a specific feature (e.g., 'leftward'), that feature is engaged in your motor plan, creating a temporary binding cost to process visual stimuli sharing that same attribute!
📌 Item 19 Categorical Perception vs Embodied Cognition (Harnad, 1987 vs Proffitt)
🔍 Categorical Perception vs Embodied Cognition (Harnad, 1987 vs Proffitt)

Core Theoretical Principle : Categorical perception compresses perceived differences within category boundaries and expands discriminability at boundaries, whereas Proffitt's model links perceived spatial metric variations to bioenergetic action costs.

« This choice identifies the precise scientific relationship. » Identifying the exact proposition articulates the theoretical contrast: Stevan Harnad's (1987) Categorical Perception shows that sensory perceptual space is warped by symbolic and conceptual boundaries, producing within-category compression (stimuli within the same category look more similar) and between-category expansion (increased discriminability across phonetic or color boundaries). In contrast, Dennis Proffitt's embodied cognition framework posits that perceived spatial dimensions (distance, hill inclination) are continuously scaled by the organism's physiological state, bioenergetic budget, and physical fatigue.

💡 Exam Key (Dave Saint-Amour) : An elegant theoretical opposition: for Harnad (categorical perception), the mind warps sensory space through categorical boundaries (compression within categories, expansion across boundaries, e.g., phonemes or colors). For Proffitt, space is warped by the physical effort required to act (a hill appears steeper when you are exhausted)!
📌 Item 20 Reverse hierarchy theory of visual perception (Ahissar & Hochstein, 2004 ; Dave Saint-Amour)
🔍 Reverse Hierarchy Theory of Visual Perception (Ahissar & Hochstein, 2004 ; Dave Saint-Amour)

Core Theoretical Principle : Initial conscious perception accesses global scene meaning ('vision at a glance') via high-level cortical areas before descending feedback to early visual areas enables fine-grained feature analysis ('vision with scrutiny').

« This choice identifies the precise scientific relationship. » Identifying the exact proposition details the Reverse Hierarchy Theory (RHT) developed by Ahissar and Hochstein (2004) and taught by Dave Saint-Amour: bottom-up feedforward visual processing propagates from primary visual cortex (V1/V2) to higher extrastriate and temporal regions without generating explicit conscious experience. Conscious visual awareness begins at the top of the hierarchy, providing an immediate, generalized scene gist ('vision at a glance'). When detailed analysis or fine discrimination is required, top-down re-entrant attentional feedback retro-activates lower visual areas with smaller receptive fields ('vision with scrutiny').

💡 Exam Key (Dave Saint-Amour) : Keep in mind the core concept taught by Dave Saint-Amour: the 'reverse hierarchy theory'! While neural spikes ascend from V1 to higher visual cortex, conscious awareness travels in reverse: we first grasp the global scene meaning ('vision at a glance' at the top) before deploying top-down attention to inspect fine details in early visual areas ('vision with scrutiny' at the bottom)!
