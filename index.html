Core Thesis: Human thought and rationality can be physically instantiated by a digital computer if that machine operates as an automatic formal system under an interpretation where formal syntactic operations mirror truth-preserving semantic relations.

  • Hobbesian reckoning: Thought is computational manipulation of tokens ('Reasoning is but reckoning'), resolving Cartesian dualism, the homunculus fallacy, and mentalistic testability.
  • Digital immunity to error: Discrete digital tokens eliminate the exponential propagation of measurement error inherent in analog systems (the poker chips analogy).
  • Two lives of formal tokens: Tokens lead an internal syntactic life (empty markers following game rules) and an interpreted semantic life (standing for things in the world).
  • The golden motto: 'If you take care of the syntax, the semantics will take care of itself.'
  • Semantic Engines: Automatic formal systems where truth-preserving syntactic transformations guarantee semantic correctness without requiring any internal consciousness.

2. The Physical Symbol System Hypothesis (PSSH)

The Physical Symbol System Hypothesis: A physical symbol system has the necessary and sufficient means for general intelligent action.

A physical symbol system consists of a set of entities, called symbols, which are physical patterns that can occur as components of another type of entity called an expression (or symbol structure)... A symbol system also contains a collection of processes that operate on expressions to produce other expressions: processes of creation, modification, copying and destruction.

By 'necessary' we mean that any system that exhibits general intelligence will prove upon analysis to be a physical symbol system. This asserts that human thinking is symbol manipulation in a physical biological brain.

By 'sufficient' we mean that any physical symbol system of sufficient size can be organized further to exhibit general intelligence. This establishes the theoretical claim that artificial intelligence is achievable in non-biological computers.

3. The Heuristic Search Hypothesis

The Heuristic Search Hypothesis: The solutions to problems are represented as symbol structures. A physical symbol system exercises its intelligence in problem solving by search—that is, by generating and progressively modifying symbol structures until a solution structure is found.

Because search spaces in real-world domains grow exponentially, brute-force exhaustive search is physically impossible. Intelligence consists precisely in using domain heuristics to prune the search tree, guiding the search directly toward plausible solution states.

📋 Conceptual Executive Summary & Key Takeaways — Hubert Dreyfus (1972/1992) — Les Limites Fondamentales de l'IA Symbolique

Core Thesis: Classical symbolic AI rests upon four unproven dogmas (biological, psychological, epistemological, ontological), running directly into the combinatorial wall of the Frame Problem. Human intelligence is not formal calculation on symbols ('knowing-that'), but embodied situational coping ('knowing-how') grounded in the perception-action cycle (Gibson, Merleau-Ponty).

  • The 4 Dogmatic Assumptions: Biological, psychological, epistemological, and ontological fallacies.
  • The Frame Problem: Inability of deductive systems to bound relevance and track invariants dynamically.
  • Embodied Coping (Knowing-How): Immediate situational engagement bypassing propositional reasoning.
  • Chinese Room & Symbol Grounding: Syntax cannot generate authentic semantics without physical anchoring.
  • Gibson's Ecological Perception: Direct pick-up of affordances guiding motor action.
  • Contemporary Paradigm Shift: Towards embodied cognition, connectionism, and active inference.
📄 Thesis I Dreyfus (1972) — The Four Dogmas

1. Deconstructing the Four Dogmas of Classical AI

In his seminal critique, Hubert Dreyfus demonstrated that GOFAI relied on four unproven Cartesian presuppositions:

  • (1) The biological assumption: That the brain operates as an on-off digital computer manipulating binary pulses. (2) The psychological assumption: That the mind operates by manipulating discrete symbols according to formal rules. (3) The epistemological assumption: That all human knowledge can be formalized as explicit propositional axioms. (4) The ontological assumption: That the real world consists of a determinate set of discrete, context-free atomic facts.
📄 Thesis II McCarthy & Hayes — The Frame Problem

2. Combinatorial Explosion and the Frame Problem

The Frame Problem (McCarthy & Hayes, 1969) highlights the radical inability of formal deductive systems to determine what remains unchanged and what is contextually relevant following an action in the environment, precipitating a catastrophic combinatorial explosion.

📄 Thesis III Heidegger & Merleau-Ponty — Embodied Coping

3. Bodily Anchoring and Tacit Know-How

Human intelligence is not disembodied theoretical calculation over propositional assertions ('knowing-that'), but direct bodily engagement in the world ('everyday coping' or 'knowing-how'), where an organism acts directly without building an explicit internal symbolic model.

📄 Thesis IV Searle & Harnad — Symbol Grounding

4. The Chinese Room & Symbol Grounding

John Searle's Chinese Room (1980) and Stevan Harnad's Symbol Grounding Problem (1990) demonstrate that purely formal syntactic manipulation within a closed system can never produce genuine intrinsic meaning without causal sensorimotor grounding in the real world.

James J. Gibson's ecological psychology (direct affordances) and active perception (Held & Hein, Bach-y-Rita) establish that perception is not a passive input calculation feeding a symbolic black box, but active information pick-up to guide motor action.

This fundamental shift explains why cognitive science transitioned from rigid symbolic functionalism to connectionism, embodied cognition, and predictive processing under active inference.
